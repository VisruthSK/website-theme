THIS CONTENT IS ENTIRELY LLM WRITTEN

The entries on this example site are stories whose copyright has expired, taken from Project Gutenberg. They stand in for the writing a real site would hold, and they were chosen because they are short enough to read in one sitting and long enough to fill several pages of justified text. This entry is the only one written for the site, and it explains the choices.

## Why old stories

Writing that is in the public domain can be copied, reset and republished without asking anyone, which makes it the natural material for a theme that is shared in the open. Old stories also happen to suit the theme. They are made of long paragraphs of plain prose, with dialogue and a little punctuation, which is exactly what a line breaker and a hanging punctuation rule have to handle well. A story by Poe tests the long sentence, and a Grimm tale tests the short one.

## What to look for

Open any of the stories on a wide screen and look at the right edge of the text. Lines end at the same place, and the spaces between words are almost the same length from one line to the next. A closing quotation mark or a full stop hangs a little way into the margin, so that the edge of the letters, not the edge of the box, lines up.

On a phone the same page is set by the browser instead. The theme does not justify text on narrow screens, because there is too little room on a line to do it well, and a ragged edge reads better than uneven gaps.

## The notes

The footnotes on the Poe story appear in the margin on a wide screen and at the foot of the page on a narrow one. Both come from the same markup, and no script is needed to move them. Each one links back to the sentence it belongs to.

## Where the text came from

Each story carries a line saying where its text came from, and the translations are the older English ones that are themselves out of copyright. Anything that is not a story, such as this entry and the colophon, was written by a language model and says so at the top.

## Reading the example

Because the stories are real stories, the example also shows how the theme treats everyday text rather than text made up to test it. Names, numbers, dashes and quotation marks all appear in their ordinary places. Nothing has been simplified to make the layout look better, so a page that looks good here should look good with your own writing. If a paragraph ever looks wrong, the same paragraph is a ready-made test to keep, and the example site is the place to add it.

The stories can be replaced at any time. Delete the files in the entries folder, add your own Markdown files with a title, a date and a description, and the same layout, typesetting and notes apply to them without any further change.
